The modern educational system, inherited from the Industrial Revolution, relies on a mass production model: one teacher for thirty students, the same textbook for everyone, and a standardized, imposed pace. This "one-size-fits-all" model is profoundly inefficient, leaving struggling students behind while hindering the potential of the most gifted.

In 1984, educational psychologist Benjamin Bloom highlighted the **"Two Sigma Problem"**: a student receiving 1-to-1 tutoring performs two standard deviations (two sigmas) better than a student in a traditional class. In other words, they outperform 98% of the students in the standard group. The problem, until today, was that individual tutoring was too expensive to be deployed at a societal scale.

Generative Artificial Intelligence has just solved Bloom's problem. Unlimited individual tutoring is now accessible for the price of an internet subscription.

## The Omniscient Synthetic Tutor

The new educational agents (based on models like GPT-4o or Gemini) are not mere interactive encyclopedias. They are **multimodal, patient, and adaptive**.

Imagine a geometry tutor that "sees" what the student is drawing on their tablet via the camera.
- If the student gets stuck on the Pythagorean theorem, the AI doesn't just give them the answer.
- It analyzes the exact nature of the mathematical error.
- It detects if the student is passionate about soccer or video games, and instantly generates a personalized problem: *"Imagine your favorite player takes a penalty kick from point X to the top corner Y, what is the distance traveled by the ball?"*

## The Staggering Acceleration of the Learning Curve

The results from early experimental cohorts (like the Khan Academy's Khanmigo initiative) are astounding.

The time required to master a complex concept—whether it's integral calculus, Python programming, or English grammar—is cut to a third. Children who benefit from these virtual tutors aren't just learning "better", they are learning **faster**.

This hyper-acceleration raises a societal question: what do we do with a 10-year-old child who, propelled by their AI, has reached a university level in mathematics, but still possesses the emotional maturity of a child?

## The New Human Role: From Teacher to Mentor

Faced with this revolution, the teaching profession is confronting an existential crisis. If an iPad app can teach photosynthesis better, more clearly, and more patiently than a biologist, what is the purpose of school?

The answer lies in human friction. AI excels at transmitting hard knowledge (Hard Skills). The human teacher must mutate to become a **socio-emotional mentor**.

The school of tomorrow will no longer be a silent lecture hall, but a laboratory for non-technical skills (Soft Skills):
- Collaboration and teamwork.
- Critical thinking in the face of AI hallucinations.
- Conflict resolution, empathy, and leadership.

While it is true that [AIs are also beginning to meddle in our intimate and psychological sphere](/articles-en/10-societe), true moral authority and inspiration will remain (for a time) the exclusive domain of humanity.

Education will no longer consist of filling a brain with facts, but of teaching a mind how to use the most powerful machines ever created to solve the problems of tomorrow.
