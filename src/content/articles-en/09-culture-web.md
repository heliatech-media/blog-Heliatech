A few years ago, the "Dead Internet" theory was merely an urban legend discussed in the depths of obscure forums like 4chan. The premise was simple and paranoid: since around 2016, a large part of the Internet has actually been populated by bots interacting with each other, creating an illusion of human activity to manipulate public opinion or generate fake engagement.

What sounded like conspiratorial science fiction is today a clinical reality. With the explosion of generative Artificial Intelligence, the Internet as we knew it—a global network of humans sharing information—is clinically dead. Welcome to the era of the Synthetic Web.

## The Silent Invasion: When Bot Traffic Surpasses Human Traffic

Have you recently read a Twitter (X) thread, a YouTube comment, or a Reddit debate that seemed oddly generic, or completely off-topic yet grammatically flawless? You've just interacted with the web's new fauna.

Traffic statistics from major CDNs (Content Delivery Networks) like Cloudflare or Akamai are explicit: an overwhelming majority of global HTTP requests no longer originate from browsers used by humans, but from automated scripts, scrapers, and AI agents.

## The Economy of Synthetic Attention

Why this invasion? Because human attention is the most monetizable resource in the world. Historically, creating viral content required talent, time, and human labor. Today, the marginal cost of creating an article, an image, or a [YouTube video](/articles-en/15-creation-contenu) via LLMs (Large Language Models) is close to zero.

We are witnessing a phenomenon of **Algorithmic Cannibalism**:
1. AIs generate billions of gigabytes of mediocre content (Slop).
2. Other AIs (the recommendation algorithms of TikTok or Meta) ingest this content.
3. Third-party AIs (engagement bots) interact with this content.

Social platforms are turning into ghost servers where bots program content for other bots, generating artificial ad revenue or influencing elections, without a single human being genuinely involved in the thought process.

## The Authenticity Crash and the Epistemological Crisis

The most profound impact of this evolution is psychological. The Internet has always been perceived as humanity's greatest public square. But if behind every username, every moral outrage, every viral trend, there potentially hides a line of code executed from an AWS server, the value of social connection collapses.

We are entering a major epistemological crisis: **the loss of proof of reality**. Cynicism becomes the default defense mechanism. Faced with a strong opinion, a shocking photo, or a scandalous video, the first question is no longer "Why does this person think that?" but "Is this a real person?".

The Turing Test is no longer an academic concept; it has become our daily digital survival mechanism.

## The Renaissance of "Dark Social": Fleeing the Open Web

How do we survive in the Dead Internet? The answer lies in a massive exodus towards *Dark Social* (closed social channels).

Users are fleeing the open, indexable, and public web (Twitter, Facebook, Google Search) to take refuge in fortified strongholds:
- Invite-only private Discord servers.
- Encrypted WhatsApp or Telegram groups.
- Paid newsletters (Substack).
- Platforms demanding cryptographic or biometric identity verification (Proof of Personhood).

The open Internet of tomorrow will likely resemble a polluted industrial wasteland of AI spam, traversed only by Google's bots desperately searching for fresh, uncorrupted training data.

The Internet is not dead; it has mutated. In a world of synthetic overabundance, the true scarcity, the ultimate luxury product, is now **the human**.
