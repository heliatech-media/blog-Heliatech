# The Dawn of the Metal Butlers

For decades, the domestic humanoid robot belonged to the realm of science fiction. The challenges associated with bipedal locomotion, delicate manipulation, and navigating unstructured environments seemed insurmountable in the short term. But everything changed with the advent of multimodal AI.

## AI: The Brain the Body Was Missing

Until recently, robotics progressed slowly, bogged down by rigid programming rules. Today, AI models capable of simultaneously processing vision, language, and physical sensors allow robots to learn through experience. They are no longer programmed to pick up a glass; they "understand" what a glass is, its fragility, and how to handle it through reinforcement learning.

Companies, including Tesla with its Optimus robot, as well as many specialized startups, are showcasing prototypes performing complex tasks: folding laundry, preparing simple meals, or tidying up a room. This physical automation extends to other critical sectors as well, as seen with [farmerless farms](/articles/19-agritech).

## Toward Mainstream Adoption

Cost remains the primary hurdle today, but the technological learning curve and economies of scale suggest that the price of a humanoid robot could soon align with that of a new car. If the promise of freeing you from all domestic chores holds true, the demand will be colossal.

## Societal Consequences

The arrival of these machines in our living rooms raises dizzying questions. What about physical and cybersecurity? The impact on personal service jobs? Even more profoundly, how will our relationship with our own private space evolve when we share it with machines possessing near-total autonomy of action? The domestic future promises to be exciting, but complex.
